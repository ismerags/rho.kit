Tracing search 42176

Someone taps your Instagram link and types a Technic set number. Here is every place that query goes, in order — and the three lines on the bill.

The short answer

You pay for one small server and nothing else. The price is fixed per month, not per search. A thousand visitors and ten visitors cost exactly the same.

Nobody's search ever touches Amazon, Flipkart or Toycra. The website only reads a database that your own Mac filled in earlier that week.

There is deliberately no arrow from the website to the retailers. If the server fetched live prices on every search, Amazon would block its datacenter address within a day, each page would take twenty seconds, and seven shops would get hammered by your visitors. Reading a database instead removes all three problems at once.

What happens, in order

Following one real set: 42176 Porsche GT4 e-Performance. Steps 1–3 happened last Sunday. Steps 4–7 take about a second.

01
Sunday 03:00 · your Mac

macOS wakes priceall on a timer. It walks all ~900 catalogue sets and asks all 7 retailers about each — roughly 5,800 requests, spread out politely over about 45 minutes.

Most answers get thrown away. Ask Toycra for 42176 and its search box happily returns 42210. The matcher demands the number actually appear in the title and rejects light kits, display stands and "compatible with" knock-offs. That discarding is the product.

Supabase · Postgres

Those rows travel over the internet into the database. Nothing is ever overwritten — "still ₹15,999 on the fortieth check" is exactly what makes a median mean something later.

About 5,800 new rows a week, ~60 MB a year. The free allowance is 500 MB, so this is comfortable for years.

Render · the web app

They type 42176. The app checks it looks like a set number — digits only, 4 to 7 of them — so nothing strange ever reaches the database. Then two indexed lookups: the summary row, and the per-retailer rows behind it.

About 20 milliseconds. No retailer is contacted. This is the only step running on hardware you rent, and it is the reason a hundred people at once feels the same as one.

What costs nothing, and why

These are the four things people usually assume they'll be billed for. None of them apply here.

₹0 — RETAILER DATA

The prices themselves

Nobody sells you this. The adapters read the same public endpoints the shops' own websites call when a normal customer loads a page. No API keys, no accounts, no subscription.

₹0 — COLLECTION

The weekly run

It happens on the Mac you already own, over the broadband you already pay for. Moving it to a server would cost money and get the server blocked, so it stays home.

₹0 — PER VISITOR

Traffic

Render's plan has no per-request charge, and the free database allowance covers roughly 50,000 page views a month. Your 500-at-peak target doesn't approach any limit.

₹0 — IMAGES

Set thumbnails

All ~900 product photos load straight from the Certified Store's own image servers. The 1 GB disk you pay ₹22 for holds only your build photos.

The one thing that can actually go wrong

If your Mac is asleep at 3 am on Sunday, that week's prices never get collected. The website keeps working perfectly — it just starts telling the truth in amber: "Last checked 12 days ago — may be out of date."

That is the price of keeping collection at home, and it is the right trade for now. The fix, if it ever annoys you, is a second small Render service running the collection on a schedule — about ₹620 more a month, and it accepts the risk that Amazon blocks a datacenter address. Or you plug the Mac in on Saturday night.
